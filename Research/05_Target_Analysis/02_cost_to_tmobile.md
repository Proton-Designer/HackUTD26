# What scams and fraud cost T-Mobile, by problem type and by segment

Prepared 2026-11-07 for the HackUTD 2026 Safe Signal team. This covers the value-to-T-Mobile side only.
Labels: **[P]** primary source (SEC, FCC, T-Mobile, FTC, court or AG). **[S]** secondary source. **[E]** our estimate or arithmetic, with the method stated. **[R]** carried over from the team's earlier research files and not re-verified here.

## 0. Read this first

1. **T-Mobile does not publish a fraud-loss line.** The 10-K and 10-Q never break out SIM-swap, scam or fraud losses. Bad debt is disclosed, but the industry itself says fraud hides inside bad debt (CFCA, row F below). Every "annual cost" below is therefore either a lumpy one-time event or an [E].
2. **The big dollars are in breach aftermath and regulatory exposure, not in day-to-day scams.**
3. **For scam calls and texts, T-Mobile pays almost nothing directly.** The victim bears the loss. T-Mobile's cost is network spend, care load, churn and regulatory pressure.
4. **New since the earlier files: the Washington AG case turned against T-Mobile in July 2026.** It is not in the earlier cost summary. See row B3.
5. **New since the earlier files: T-Mobile's own support pages say SIM Protection is "Not available to T-Mobile for Business, T-Mobile Prepaid, or Metro by T-Mobile."** Metro's own page says SIM Change Protection is free to all Metro customers. The two pages disagree. See section 3.

### Bottom line on direct cost
Documented T-Mobile direct hits over five years: about $400M (2021 settlement charge) + $31.5M (FCC) + $60M (CFIUS) + $33M (Jones) = **about $525M**, plus open Washington exposure (about $72M at a low-end illustration). The earlier cost summary quoted about $625M; that does not reconcile with these components, so use about $525M. Do not present a per-year number as fact.

## 5. Where T-Mobile loses the most

1. **Breach aftermath is the largest realized cost** (about $400M + $31.5M + $60M), and the open Washington AG judgment could add tens of millions or more. It was about how T-Mobile told customers (text-message notices), not just that data leaked. **Post-incident communication is itself a loss driver.**
2. **A single SIM-swap case ($33M) cost more than the FCC fine ($31.5M).** Account takeover is rare now but extremely expensive per event, and the path is insiders and compromised credentials.
3. **Churn is the largest unquantified cost.** One basis point of postpaid account churn is about $78M of annual revenue. T-Mobile's churn is already rising, and nobody has published how much of it fraud causes.
4. **Bad debt ($1.26B in nine months, up 36%) may contain fraud.** The industry says subscription and first-party fraud hides there. T-Mobile does not break it out. If the team can say anything credible here, it is a question for T-Mobile judges, not a claim.
5. **Scam calls and texts are cheap for T-Mobile directly but are the largest-volume problem** (3B texts, 29B calls). Their cost to T-Mobile is care load, brand and regulatory heat.
