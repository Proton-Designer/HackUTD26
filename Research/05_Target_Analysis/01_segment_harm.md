# Agent 1: Customer harm by segment (Safe Signal / T-Mobile)

Compiled 2026-11-07. Scope: harm to the customer only (not company cost).

**Labels.** `[S#]` = sourced, URL in the source list at the bottom. `(calc.)` = arithmetic on sourced numbers. `(est.)` = my estimate or inference, not in a source. Anything without one of these labels should be treated as unsourced.

**Read this first: data hygiene**
- FTC Sentinel = self-reported complaints (not a survey). FBI IC3 = a separate complaint stream (partly feeds FTC). Do not add them together. [S8][S4]
- FTC says only about 46% of 2025 fraud reports include age, and an unreported-loss multiplier is large (FTC modeled $261.5B all-ages 2025, $119.5B for 60+ at the high end; $38.1B / $13.3B at the "very conservative" end). [S1]
- No FTC Consumer Sentinel Data Book 2025 PDF exists yet in what I could reach. Full 2025 age/contact tables are therefore not available. Latest full edition = 2024. 2025 figures come from the FTC older-adults report (Oct 2026), FTC spotlights, FTC testimony, and the IC3 2025 report. [S3][S1][S4]
- Corrections to the team's existing file `03-scam-data.md`: (1) "text losses +61%" is **older adults only** [S1]; all-ages text losses went $470M (2024) to $639M (2025), about +36% (calc., 2025 figure via secondary source [S7]). (2) The odd contact-method shares (website/app 31%, social 28%, phone 11%, email 10%, text 7%) are shares of **loss reports by count**, not dollars. Social media is #1 by **dollars** ($2.1B). [S6] (3) The FTC imposter alert prints romance losses as "$1.48 million" with "$2,020 per person"; "million" is almost certainly a typo for billion, but I could not verify. [S10]

## 2. Top 5 segments (short paragraphs)

**1. Older adults 60+ (and 80+ especially).**
This is the largest and best-documented pool of harm. The FTC counts about $3B reported in 2025 (up from $2.4B), and the IC3 counts $7.748B, 37% of all IC3 dollars from 20% of complaints. [S1][S4] Loss *rate* is lower than for the young (74% of older adults' reports show no loss, and they file no-loss reports at a 34% higher rate), so they detect and report scams more often. [S1] The harm is concentrated in a few giant losses: 6% of 60+ loss reports are $100K+ and make up 70% of dollars. [S1] Phone is where they lose the most per person ($3,000 median vs $599 for social), and 80+ lose mainly by phone. [S1] IC3 shows 60+ account for 49% of tech-support dollars and 52% of government-impersonation dollars (calc.), plus 6,188 of the ~11,300 crypto-ATM complaints (55%, calc.). [S4] These are call-driven "move your money to protect it" chains. [S12] Recovery is poor: IC3's kill-chain froze $32.9M of $65.4M for 60+ victims in 2025, and only when reported fast. [S4] This is the clearest match for T-Mobile's control of voice and SMS.

**2. Investment / crypto-exposed adults.**
Largest dollars of any scam type: IC3 investment $8.65B and crypto-nexus $11.37B; FTC investment >$7.9B, about half of all FTC losses. [S4][S8] Per-victim damage is huge (~$118K IC3 investment average, calc.). Contact is mainly social media and messaging apps, so T-Mobile sees only the edges: the "wrong number" text opener, the later WhatsApp/SMS handoff, and fake-broker calls. [S1][S6] SIM swap itself is small (971 IC3 complaints, $17.4M, ~$17.9K average, calc.; falling from $48.8M in 2023), but it is the one crypto-holder harm that sits inside T-Mobile's own systems. [S4] Caveat: no source defines "crypto/HNW holders" as a segment, so I treat this as an exposure-based group.

**3. Romance / lonely / bereaved.**
IC3 romance losses are $929M overall and $584M for 60+, up 50% for 60+ in one year; the average 60+ romance victim lost ~$57K (calc.). [S4] About 60% of FTC romance losers say it began on social media. [S6] Widowed people were 22% of 60+ fraud victims vs 8% of all fraud victims in BJS survey data (2017, via NIJ). [S28] Research shows loneliness and low well-being raise scam susceptibility, most strongly for divorced, widowed or never-married older adults. [S34] Weakness: no 2025 source measures bereavement directly, and Javelin reports romance victims fell 41%. [S24] Treat the segment as real, but trend as uncertain.

**4. Small-business owners.**
BEC is the third-largest IC3 loss type at $3.05B (+10%), ~$123K per complaint (calc.). [S4] But it is email/invoice-fraud driven, so T-Mobile's calls/texts control is weaker here. The telco-relevant slice is account takeover, SIM swap/port-out against owners and finance staff, and carrier-impersonation vishing. ITRC says 81% of small businesses reported an attack/breach (survey). [S22] Evidence is weak: IC3 does not label "small business," and BEC victims may be larger firms. Value to T-Mobile is high (business accounts), harm evidence is medium.

**5. Job seekers.**
FTC job-scam losses rose from $90M (2020) to $501M (2024). [S14][S15] BBB reports that employment-scam reports doubled in 2025 and that half of 23,000+ reports began with a text. [S16] Task scams carry a BBB median loss of $2,300 and victims are asked to keep depositing crypto. [S16] This is the segment where the scam *starts in the SMS channel T-Mobile carries*, so detection leverage is highest. The dollars per person are smaller than for the elderly, and 2025 FTC totals are missing, so severity ranks below segments 1-4. Overlaps heavily with young adults (segment 6), where CR shows text/messaging-origin scams jumping from 13% to 40% of 18-29 scam attempts. [S19]

## 3. Channel view (what T-Mobile controls)

- All ages, 2025, FTC, share of loss reports by count: website/app 31%, social 28%, phone 11%, email 10%, text 7%. [S6]
- All ages, 2025, FTC, dollars: social $2.1B; website/app $1.1B; phone $1.1B; text $639M; email $569M. [S6][S7] Phone+text = ~32% of the five contact-method dollar totals (calc.).
- 2024 FTC: text $470M (11% of text reports had a loss); phone and text were #2 and #3 by report count behind email. [S3]
- Older adults: phone dominates for 80+; text scams are the fastest-growing older-adult channel (+61%). [S1]
- Young adults: social first, text/messaging fastest-growing (CR). [S6][S19]
- Texas relevance (hackathon in DFW): Texas was #2 in IC3 complaints (97,912) and #3 in 60+ losses ($678.6M). [S4]

## 4. Data gaps / caveats

1. **No FTC 2025 Data Book.** Age-by-contact-method, age-by-payment and per-category 2025 medians are unavailable. The 2024 age table in the Data Book PDF extracted garbled; I used only the headline numbers (20-29 44% loss rate, 70-79 24%, 80+ 21%; medians $417 / $1,000 / $1,650). [S3][S32]
2. **FTC vs IC3 differ by design.** FTC median 60+ ($900) and IC3 average 60+ ($38,500) are a median of loss reports vs a mean of all complaints (IC3 divides total dollars by all complaints, including some with no loss; verified for the all-ages $20,699 figure). Do not compare them. [S4]
3. **IC3 "phishing/spoofing" 60+ complaints jumped 2,856 (2023) to 23,252 (2024) to 48,064 (2025) while all-ages complaints fell.** This looks like a reclassification, not real growth. Do not cite as a trend. [S4]
4. **Segments with no usable US quantitative data:** people with disabilities, immigrants/limited-English (only qualitative FTC 2021, AARP 2021 and CR/Pew survey gaps), recently bereaved, prepaid/Metro customers, students as distinct from 18-29, T-Mobile-customer-specific victimization (no carrier-level data exists publicly). Treat rows 8, 10 and "prepaid" as hypotheses.
5. **Reporting bias cuts both ways.** Older adults report no-loss attempts at higher rates; younger, lower-income and minority victims report less and feel blamed more (23.7% of under-35 vs 8.7% of 75+ felt blamed; 28.7% of Black, 9.7% of White) [S20][S1]. FTC underreporting rates: ~2.0% (losses <$1,000) and 6.7% (>$1,000) by its own estimate. [S1]
6. **Survey vs complaint numbers differ by an order of magnitude.** UAS: 33% of adults (87.5M) experienced a scam in the prior year, $42.7B unrefunded, average ~$2,400, and at least 30% of those who lost money could not recover it. [S20] Javelin: 18M scam victims, $10.7B. [S24] CFPB (UAS): 32M (34%) of 60+ experienced fraud, 3.7M lost money in 2024. [S21]
7. **Secondary sources used:** 2025 text/phone/email dollar totals (MOAA article on the FTC spotlight) [S7]; 2025 medians for under-60 age bands (ppc.land) [S17]; 2023 IC3 60+ crypto $1.65B and 2022 comparisons from memory of IC3 2023 elder report (not re-fetched). The FTC 2022 total ($8.8B) is also from memory and unverified; I excluded it.
8. **Conflicts:** ITRC breach-notice "phishing increase" 53.7% (report) vs 40% (press release) [S22]. MOAA military 2024 total $548M vs FTC $584M. [S7][S3] The FTC romance figure "$1.48 million" is likely a typo. [S10]
9. **The "most vulnerable x most valuable" call is not made here** (harm only). Segments 1, 5 and 6 are where harm happens over channels T-Mobile controls; segments 2 and 4 are the biggest dollars but sit mostly on social/email.
