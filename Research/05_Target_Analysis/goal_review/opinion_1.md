# Opinion 1: Primary T-Mobile goal for Safe Signal

**1. Primary: Goal 6, Customer trust and security.**
Reasons:
- The brief IS this goal. "Protect customers from scams and fraud" maps directly to Goal 6's facts (nearly 50% more scam/spam messages in 2025, ~3B scam texts blocked, Scam Shield, "preparing for AI-enabled fraud"). Judges are T-Mobile employees scoring what they see, so no translation layer is needed.
- Judging criteria "security and privacy" and "impact" are Goal 6 vocabulary, and "privacy by design" appears in the build spec. The breach history ($350M settlement, $31.5M FCC decree) gives the pitch real stakes.
- "The big unlock: customers feel protected before, during and after a threat" is a trust outcome. It lets us design a full lifecycle (prevention, real-time, recovery) rather than a classifier, which is what "go beyond classification" asks for.

**2. Secondary: Goal 4, Cut cost through digital and AI.** Use only as the business rationale in one pitch slide: each scam handled in-app (T-Life, ~24M MAU) is a call to care avoided, supporting the ~$3B digitalization/AI savings target. Fraud-driven care calls are a cost; our experience deflects them. Keep it a single line of ROI, not a design driver.

**3. Avoid as framing:**
- Goal 1 (revenue per account): scam protection as upsell contradicts "protect customers" and risks reading as monetizing fear after price-increase backlash.
- Goal 2 (win/keep accounts): plausible but vague; churn claims can't be demonstrated in a 4-minute demo with synthetic data.
- Goal 3 (differentiation): too generic, and every feature claims it.
- Goal 5 (new businesses): no link to scams in the brief.
- Goal 7 (AI platform/AI-RAN): network-layer story we can't build or demo in 24 hours; tempting only via the NVIDIA track.

**4. Strongest argument against:** Goal 6 is the obvious choice, so every team will anchor there and we won't stand out; HackUTD 2025's winners were PM-facing dashboards (Customer Happiness Index) that spoke in business-metric language, suggesting judges reward a clear business-outcome tie. Why I hold it anyway: the brief explicitly defines the product as scam protection, and differentiation should come from creativity and execution (lifecycle experience, honest confidence, privacy), which are scored criteria. The secondary Goal 4 covers the business-metric appeal. Also, DubHacks '26 results (anti-scam device placed 3rd, with firsts going to other themes) show the sponsor is open to variety, so a tight, well-demoed Goal 6 entry is safe and high-floor.

**5. Confidence:** High on primary (Goal 6); medium on Goal 4 as secondary (Goal 2 is a reasonable alternate).
