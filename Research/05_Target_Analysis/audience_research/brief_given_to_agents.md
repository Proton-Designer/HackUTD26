# Shared brief for audience research (HackUTD 2026, T-Mobile "Safe Signal")

Today: Nov 7, 2026. A 4-person student team, 24-hour hackathon (submissions due Sun Nov 8, noon).

## Locked goal (do not change)
**Customers are protected, and feel protected, before, during and after a scam. That trust is what keeps them with T-Mobile.**
(Primary = T-Mobile goal "customer trust and security"; business reason = keeping accounts; proof metric = scams resolved without a care call + detection accuracy.)

## T-Mobile prompt (verbatim)
Safe Signal: Protect customers from scams and fraud in ways we haven't thought of yet.
- You build: Any approach: prevention, real-time protection, education, new security features. Scam detection is a core piece, not the whole product. Go beyond classification: safeguards and customer-facing experiences. Clear next steps, honest confidence, privacy by design.
- You create the data: synthetic scam messages and scenarios only. No real customer data.
- Judges look for: Creativity, impact, usability, security and privacy, detection quality.
- The big unlock: Customers feel protected before, during and after a threat.
(Second track: NVIDIA "Use Nemotron Beyond a Chatbot": multi-step agent workflows, tool integration, real-world applicability.)

## What "highest leverage audience" means here
A specific, niche group of **T-Mobile customers** (not "everyone", not "seniors" in general) where protecting them moves the goal the most. Score on:
1. Harm: money lost per victim × how often they're hit (real numbers)
2. Vulnerability: can they spot it, report it, recover (before/during/after)
3. Channel: the scam runs through what T-Mobile controls (calls, texts, the account/number, T-Mobile brand impersonation)
4. Gap: existing tools (Scam Shield, phone OS, banks, third-party apps) fail them, with evidence
5. Value to T-Mobile: account value, churn risk, care cost, brand/regulatory exposure (tie-breaker)
6. Demo-able in 24h with synthetic data, and a judge would immediately "get it"
Must be **unique but genuinely valuable**: a niche chosen because the data says it's where harm and leverage concentrate, not novelty for its own sake. The obvious pick ("all seniors") is allowed only if narrowed to a sharper slice the data supports.
